# AI-Assisted University Course, Exam and Room Timetabling — State of the Art (2026)

*Research report for the engineering team building the new scheduling/optimizer module. Compiled 2026-10-07 from web sources; every claim is cited inline and collected under "Sources". Items marked **[unverified]** could not be confirmed against a primary source and should be re-checked before being relied on.*

## Executive summary

1. **The problem is well-studied and our instance is small by competition standards.** ITC-2019 instances (real UniTime data) have thousands of classes, hundreds of rooms, 288 five-minute slots per day and 13–16 weeks; the winning approach was a MIP-based fix-and-optimize matheuristic, 2nd was pure MIP with preprocessing, 3rd was simulated annealing ([ITC 2019 winners](https://www.apereo.org/news/2020/unitime-2019-competition-winners); [Ceschia et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.07525)). 1,500 sections × 60 rooms × 6 days × ~20 slots is well within reach of exact methods.
2. **Room assignment alone is easy; the coupling with time is what is hard.** For fixed times, each period is a bipartite matching (polynomial); the moment a class must stay in one room over consecutive periods the problem is NP-hard even for two periods ([Carter & Tovey 1992](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/opre.40.1.S28); [Phillips et al. 2015](https://www.academia.edu/50372604/Integer_programming_methods_for_large_scale_practical_classroom_assignment_problems)). Our workflow (departments propose day/time, planner assigns rooms) sits mostly on the easy side, which is why a 100 %-hard-feasible target is realistic.
3. **Google OR-Tools CP-SAT (v9.15, Jan 2026) is the right core engine**: it won gold in Fixed/Free/Parallel at the MiniZinc Challenge 2025 and 2026, has optional interval variables + `NoOverlap`/`Cumulative`, assumptions for infeasibility cores, solution hints for warm starts and built-in LNS/feasibility-jump workers ([MiniZinc 2025](https://minizinc.org/challenge/2025/results); [MiniZinc 2026](https://minizinc.org/challenge/2026/results); [OR-Tools releases](https://github.com/google/or-tools/releases); [CP-SAT Primer](https://d-krupke.github.io/cpsat-primer/parameters.html)). A 2026 paper solved a 6,278-lecture Brazilian university instance with room-wise CP-SAT decomposition in 4.45 s ([Moreira & Freitas 2026](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/semish/article/view/43522)).
4. **Infeasibility explanation is a solved-enough engineering problem, but CP-SAT's native core is only a starting point.** `sufficient_assumptions_for_infeasibility()` returns a non-minimal core, requires `num_workers=1`, and misbehaves with an objective; use it to seed a QuickXplain/deletion-based MUS loop, and pair it with a "slack + lexicographic minimisation" diagnostic model that always returns a schedule plus a ranked list of violations ([OR-Tools troubleshooting](https://github.com/google/or-tools/blob/stable/ortools/sat/docs/troubleshooting.md); [or-tools discussion #3630](https://github.com/google/or-tools/discussions/3630); [CPMpy explain tools](https://cpmpy.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api/tools/explain.html); [Rodler 2022 on QuickXplain](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.01835)).
5. **Do not let an LLM solve or directly edit the timetable.** The 2026 benchmarks are brutal: average hard-feasibility of LLM-produced schedules is 15.2 % on SCHEDBench (best model 55.9 %) and the best agent passes 35.5 % of end-to-end OR tasks on ORAgentBench ([SCHEDBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00991v1); [ORAgentBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19787)). The LLM's job is to translate natural language into a *typed constraint diff* that the solver applies.
6. **The pattern that works is "LLM → structured constraint IR → deterministic compilation → solver → LLM-worded explanation"**, as in OptiMUS, ORPilot's solver-agnostic IR, MeetMate's verified constraint functions and TRACE-CS's logic+LLM explanations ([OptiMUS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10172); [ORPilot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02728); [MeetMate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06908); [TRACE-CS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/35374)). Claude's structured outputs / strict tool use give schema-guaranteed JSON for this ([Claude structured outputs docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)).
7. **Minimum-perturbation re-solving is a first-class requirement, not a nice-to-have.** The literature (Müller/Rudová/Barták 2004–05, Phillips et al. 2017, Lemos et al. 2020/2021, Akkan et al. 2021) and UniTime's "MPP mode" all treat "change as little as possible" as a separate objective with its own solver mode ([Müller et al.](https://www.ics.muni.cz/en/research/publications/621512); [Lemos et al. 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jsched/v24y2021i1d10.1007_s10951-020-00666-3.html); [UniTime exam manual](https://help.unitime.org/manuals/examination-timetabling)).
8. **Exam timetabling has its own vocabulary we should adopt verbatim**: period/room hard constraints (conflict, capacity, duration, AFTER, COINCIDENCE, EXCLUSION, EXCLUSIVE) and the soft set (two-in-a-row, two-in-a-day, period spread, mixed durations, front-load, period/room penalties) from ITC-2007, plus UniTime's "exam seating capacity" vs normal capacity and "max rooms per exam" split ([ITC2007 exam track report](https://www.eeecs.qub.ac.uk/itc2007/examtrack/report/Exam_Track_TechReportv4.0.pdf); [UniTime exam manual](https://help.unitime.org/manuals/examination-timetabling)).
9. **Reuse the ITC-2019 XML schema and UniTime data model rather than inventing one**: it already encodes day bitstrings, week bitstrings, room options with penalties, travel times, and all distribution constraints we need ([ITC-2019 problem paper](https://www.itc2019.org/papers/itc2019-patat2018.pdf)). Export with iCalendar RFC 5545 ([RFC 5545](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc5545/)).
10. **Timefold is a credible alternative but a worse fit for us**: Timefold 2.0 (April 2026) is Java/Kotlin only — the Python port was archived on 6 Oct 2025 — and its explainability APIs (score analysis, recommended assignment) moved to the paid "Plus" edition ([Timefold 2.0 release](https://timefold.ai/blog/timefold-solver-2.0-release); [timefold-solver-python](https://github.com/TimefoldAI/timefold-solver-python)).

## 1. Problem formulations

### 1.1 Course timetabling: PE-CTT and CB-CTT (ITC-2007 tracks 2 and 3)

Two "standard" formulations dominate the literature, both from ITC-2007:

* **Post-Enrolment CTT (PE-CTT, track 2)** — events are placed into timeslots and rooms subject to student enrolments, room features and precedence. State of the art is local search: Tabu Search (Nagata 2018) and Simulated Annealing (Goh et al. 2017–2020) hold essentially all best-known results ([Ceschia, Di Gaspero & Schaerf 2022/2023 survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.07525), §4.2).
* **Curriculum-Based CTT (CB-CTT, track 3)** — courses (lectures) are placed into periods and rooms; conflicts are defined via *curricula* (groups of courses taken by the same cohort) rather than individual students. This is the formulation closest to a Turkish programme/year structure. Best known results are held by Abdullah & Turabieh (2012, GA+tabu with large-neighbourhood operators) and Kiefer, Hartl & Schnell (2017, Adaptive LNS inside Simulated Annealing) (survey §4.3; [Kiefer et al. 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/annopr/v252y2017i2d10.1007_s10479-016-2151-2.html)). On the exact side, Achá & Nieuwenhuis' SAT/MaxSAT approach reached best-known solutions on 21 of 32 instances, 19 proven optimal ([Achá & Nieuwenhuis 2014](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/annopr/v218y2014i1p71-9110.1007-s10479-012-1081-x.html)); Lach & Lübbecke and Cacchiani et al. closed many instances with IP and lower bounds. Takeaway: the ITC-2007 CB-CTT instances (30–131 courses, 5–20 rooms) are **far smaller** than ours and are "too easily solved to optimality" (survey conclusion B), so our target is not benchmark novelty but robust engineering.

### 1.2 Room assignment as a sub-problem

[Carter & Tovey (1992)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/opre.40.1.S28) settled when classroom assignment is hard: with times fixed, **each period is an independent assignment problem solvable in polynomial time as a maximum-weight bipartite matching (classes × rooms)**; but requiring a class to stay in the same room across consecutive periods ("contiguous room stability") makes it NP-hard **even for two periods** ([Phillips et al. 2015](https://www.academia.edu/50372604/Integer_programming_methods_for_large_scale_practical_classroom_assignment_problems), Computers & OR 53:42–53, which also gives a tractable generalised IP validated at the University of Auckland). The interval-scheduling view (rooms = machines, sections = intervals, feasibility = colouring the interval graph with ≤ 60 colours subject to capacity/type compatibility) explains *why* a planner-assigns-rooms workflow usually feels easy, and why it breaks when many 2-slot and 3-slot blocks compete for the few large or TIP rooms. **Design consequence:** model room assignment as per-section optional-interval variables with `NoOverlap` per room (CP-SAT), and give the planner a "re-room only" mode that keeps times fixed — that mode is near-polynomial and will be instant.

### 1.3 Examination timetabling

* **Toronto benchmark** (Carter, Laporte & Lee 1996): 13 uncapacitated real instances; objective = proximity cost of student exams. Best upper bounds are scattered across authors; all lower bounds are from Gogos et al. 2021 (survey §4.1). Good for unit-testing a conflict-spread objective, but it has **no rooms**.
* **ITC-2007 exam track** (capacitated, with rooms and periods) is the model to copy. Hard constraints: no student in two exams in one period; room capacity never exceeded; exam duration ≤ period duration; AFTER / EXAM_COINCIDENCE / EXCLUSION period rules; EXCLUSIVE room rule. Soft constraints with institutional weights: *two in a row*, *two in a day*, *period spread* (default gap 7 periods), *mixed durations* in a room, *front load* (large exams early; 3 parameters), *period penalty*, *room penalty* ([ITC2007 exam track technical report v4.0](https://www.eeecs.qub.ac.uk/itc2007/examtrack/report/Exam_Track_TechReportv4.0.pdf)). Exams may share rooms. Winner was Tomáš Müller's constraint-based solver (iterative forward search + conflict-based statistics, then hill climbing and great deluge) — the same engine that became UniTime ([Müller ITC2007 solver description](https://www.eeecs.qub.ac.uk/itc2007/winner/bestexamsolutions/itc2007_Muller.pdf); [Müller 2009](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/annopr/v172y2009i1p429-44610.1007-s10479-009-0644-y.html)). Current best results come from Bykov and co-workers' Late Acceptance / Step-Counting Hill Climbing over Kempe-chain neighbourhoods (survey §4.4).

### 1.4 ITC-2019 (UniTime-style real university data)

The formulation we should treat as our reference model ([Müller, Rudová & Müllerová, ITC-2019 problem description](https://www.itc2019.org/papers/itc2019-patat2018.pdf); journal version in [Journal of Scheduling 2024](https://muni.cz/en/research/publications/2352558)):

* `<problem nrDays="7" nrWeeks="13" slotsPerDay="288">` — time is 5-minute slots over the whole day; classes have a list of `<time days="1010100" start="90" length="10" weeks="1111111111111" penalty="20"/>` options (bitstrings for days and weeks) and `<room id penalty/>` options; rooms carry `capacity`, `<unavailable .../>` blocks and pairwise `<travel room value/>` in slots.
* Courses → configurations → subparts → classes, with `parent` links so student sectioning is consistent.
* Distribution constraints (hard via `required="true"` or soft via `penalty`): SameStart, SameTime, DifferentTime, SameDays, DifferentDays, SameWeeks, DifferentWeeks, Overlap, NotOverlap, SameRoom, DifferentRoom, SameAttendees (uses travel times), Precedence, WorkDay(S), MinGap(G), MaxDays(D), MaxDayLoad(S), MaxBreaks(R,S), MaxBlock(M,S).
* Objective = weighted sum `<optimization time="2" room="1" distribution="1" student="2"/>`.

**Winning approaches:** 1st Holm, Mikkelsen, Sørensen & Stidsen (DTU/MaCom) with a graph-based MIP and a parallel fix-and-optimize matheuristic — best solutions on 29/30 instances, but only 5 solved to proven optimality ([Holm et al. 2022](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jsched/v25y2022i4d10.1007_s10951-022-00728-8.html); [DTU cutting-plane report](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/a-cutting-plane-algorithm-for-the-international-timetabling-compe/)); 2nd Rappos et al. (HEIG-VD) with MIP plus preprocessing; 3rd Gashi & Sylejmani (Prishtina) with simulated annealing — best open-source award, code at [github.com/edongashi/itc-2019](https://github.com/edongashi/itc-2019); 5th Lemos, Monteiro & Lynce with MaxSAT ([Apereo results](https://www.apereo.org/news/2020/unitime-2019-competition-winners); survey §4.6). The site still accepts new solutions and hosts a validator ([itc2019.org](https://www.itc2019.org/)).

### 1.5 ITC-2021 (sports)

Double round-robin tournaments for 16–20 teams in the RobinX XML format. Final ranking: 1st Team UoS (Southampton: Martínez-Sykora, Potts, Lamas-Fernández, 596 pts), 2nd Udine (Rosati, Petris, Di Gaspero, Schaerf — three-stage multi-neighbourhood simulated annealing, feasible on 44/45 instances), 3rd HSE "Saturn", 4th GOAL (Fonseca & Toffolo — IP fix-and-optimize, 37/45 feasible), 5th ZIB MODAL, 6th TU/e ([ITC2021 results](https://robinxval.ugent.be/ITC2021/); [Rosati et al. 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10951-022-00740-y); [Van Bulck & Goossens EJOR 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ejores/v308y2023i3p1249-1267.html)). The Southampton method is described as a fix-and-relax ILP matheuristic ([Southampton eprint](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/474442)) **[unverified that this eprint is the exact competition entry]**. Relevance to us is indirect: the lesson across ITC-2019 and ITC-2021 is the same — **MIP/CP matheuristics (fix-and-optimize = LNS with an exact sub-solver) beat pure metaheuristics when rules are rich and commercial/exact solvers are allowed.**

## 2. Exact and hybrid solvers

### 2.1 Google OR-Tools CP-SAT

* **Version:** v9.15, released 12 Jan 2026 (PyPI `ortools 9.15.6755`, 14 Jan 2026). v9.12–9.15 brought faster LNS, faster feasibility-jump and violation local search, hints preserved through presolve, `no_overlap_2d` and precedence improvements, enforcement literals on more constraints, and LRAT proof logging ([OR-Tools releases](https://github.com/google/or-tools/releases); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/ortools/)).
* **Competition standing:** gold in Fixed, Free and Parallel at MiniZinc Challenge 2025 (the CP-SAT LS variant took Local Search gold) and gold in Fixed/Free/Parallel/Open in 2026 ([2025 results](https://minizinc.org/challenge/2025/results); [2026 results](https://minizinc.org/challenge/2026/results)).
* **Relevant features:** `new_optional_interval_var` + `add_no_overlap` (one per room), `add_cumulative` (e.g. invigilator pool or seat budget across split rooms), `only_enforce_if` enforcement literals, `add_assumptions` + `sufficient_assumptions_for_infeasibility()`, `add_hint` for warm starts (`fix_variables_to_their_hinted_value=True` to *verify* a manual edit is feasible; `keep_all_feasible_solutions_in_presolve` if hints get lost), `num_workers` portfolio (at ≥2 workers incomplete LNS/feasibility-jump subsolvers join; `use_lns_only=True` gives a pure repair mode under a time limit), `max_time_in_seconds`, `relative_gap_limit` ([CP-SAT Primer – parameters](https://d-krupke.github.io/cpsat-primer/parameters.html); [Python reference](https://developers.google.com/optimization/reference/python/sat/python/cp_model)).
* **Objectives:** CP-SAT has a single linear objective; lexicographic optimisation is done by solving in stages and fixing each level (the standard method, see [IBM CP Optimizer multi-objective docs](https://ibm.com/docs/en/SSSA5P_22.1.1/ilog.odms.cpo.help/refcppcpoptimizer/html/multiobj.html) for the semantics) or by widely spaced integer weights. Hints from level *k* seed level *k+1*.
* **Known sharp edges:** assumptions require `num_workers=1`, the returned set is "minimized but not guaranteed to be minimal", and combining assumptions with an objective can return *all* assumptions ([troubleshooting.md](https://github.com/google/or-tools/blob/stable/ortools/sat/docs/troubleshooting.md); [discussion #3630](https://github.com/google/or-tools/discussions/3630); [issue #3983](https://github.com/google/or-tools/issues/3983)). The solver is stateless (no incremental re-solve across calls), so "fast re-solve" must come from hints + LNS, not from solver state.

**Sizing for our instance.** A naïve `x[section, day, slot, room]` Boolean grid is ~1,500 × 6 × 20 × 60 ≈ 10.8 M variables — avoid it. Follow ITC-2019 instead: enumerate per section the *feasible time patterns* (requested day/start, or a handful of alternatives) and the *compatible rooms* (capacity ≥ students, building/type match, TIP/PC-lab flags). Typical fan-out is ≤ 50 time options × ≤ 15 rooms, i.e. ≈ 10⁵ Booleans plus one optional interval per (section, room) and 60 `NoOverlap` constraints. Weeks are handled by only generating pairwise disjunctions for sections whose week bitmasks intersect (sections in weeks 1–7 and 8–14 never conflict). This is the same scale that [Moreira & Freitas (2026)](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/semish/article/view/43522) solve per room in seconds (6,278 lectures, 4.45 s, 100 % coverage; caveat: their "controlled fallback" may create instructor conflicts, which we must *not* accept). Expect sub-minute full solves and sub-second re-rooming.

### 2.2 MIP: HiGHS, SCIP, Gurobi

* **Gurobi** has mature IIS (`computeIIS`), IIS "force" attributes to steer the explanation toward user-editable constraints, and warns that IIS on MIPs is expensive and not guaranteed minimal ([Gurobi IIS support article](https://support.gurobi.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029969391); [computeIIS reference](https://gurobi.com/documentation/11.0/refman/cs_model_computeiis.html)). Commercial licence; Gurobi withdrew from Mittelmann's public benchmarks in Aug 2024, so there is no neutral current comparison ([Mittelmann benchmarks](https://plato.asu.edu/bench.html)).
* **HiGHS** (open source) — its IIS facility is "under development and only available for LPs"; a MathOptIIS.jl user reported >30 min for cases Gurobi handled in seconds; multithreaded MIP was slated for v1.15 (June 2026) **[unverified whether shipped]** ([HiGHS docs via GAMS](https://gams.com/54/docs/S_HIGHS.html); [JuMP-dev 2026 slides](https://jump.dev/assets/jump-dev-workshops/2026/slides_julian.pdf); [MathOptIIS issue](https://github.com/jump-dev/MathOptIIS.jl/issues/20)).
* **SCIP** 9.2.x (Jan 2025) is a strong academic MIP/CIP solver; no built-in IIS found ([scipopt.org](https://scipopt.org)).

MIP matheuristics won ITC-2019 and ITC-2021, but for a product with sub-minute interactive re-solves and rich logical constraints (pairwise room/time incompatibilities, weeks, "2 large OR 3 small") CP-SAT's propagation and LNS workers are a better fit, and we avoid licence cost. Keep a MIP path only if we later need proven lower bounds for reporting.

### 2.3 Timefold / OptaPlanner

Timefold Solver 2.0 shipped in April 2026 (delayed from March): new declarative Neighborhoods API, full list-variable support, Constraint Streams with precomputed joins and constraint profiling, Apache-2.0 core; **score analysis and "recommended assignment" (the explainability APIs) are now in the paid Plus edition** (free for non-profit/academic use), and 1.x gets quarterly fixes through 2026 ([Timefold 2.0 release](https://timefold.ai/blog/timefold-solver-2.0-release); [GitHub discussion #2036](https://github.com/TimefoldAI/timefold-solver/discussions/2036)). The default algorithm stack is a construction heuristic followed by local search (Late Acceptance / Tabu / SA) — strong on soft-score quality but, unlike CP-SAT, it **cannot prove infeasibility or produce cores**; it reports hard-score violations after the fact. The school-timetabling quickstart (Timeslot, Room, Lesson with `timeslot`/`room` planning variables; room/teacher/student-group conflicts hard; room stability, time efficiency, subject variety soft) is a good reference domain model ([quickstart model](https://docs.timefold.ai/timefold-solver/1.x/quickstart/shared/school-timetabling/school-timetabling-model)). **The Python solver was archived 6 Oct 2025**; the community fork "SolverForge" claims compatibility but is not affiliated ([timefold-solver-python](https://github.com/TimefoldAI/timefold-solver-python); [solverforge-quickstarts](https://github.com/solverforge/solverforge-quickstarts)). Using Timefold would therefore mean a JVM service.

### 2.4 UniTime's solver

Iterative Forward Search over partial feasible assignments with conflict-based statistics, followed by hill climbing and great deluge; supports room-only / time-only / both change modes, "Suggestions" that look for alternatives affecting ≤ 2 other classes, scenario testing, and an MPP mode for exams that "also minimizes changes to an existing timetable" ([UniTime course timetabling](https://help.unitime.org/course-timetabling); [UniTime exam manual](https://help.unitime.org/manuals/examination-timetabling)). Open source (Apereo), Java. It is the most complete *product* reference but is a monolith we would not embed.

### 2.5 MiniZinc

Useful as a model-prototyping and cross-solver validation layer (the same `.mzn` can be run on CP-SAT, Chuffed, Gecode, HiGHS); CP-SAT is the top MiniZinc back end anyway. Not recommended as the production API surface because of FlatZinc compile overhead on every re-solve.

**Verdict:** Python + OR-Tools CP-SAT, with MiniZinc only for offline model validation, and Timefold/UniTime as UX and data-model references.

## 3. Infeasibility explanation

Three complementary mechanisms, in increasing cost:

1. **Assumption cores (fast, coarse).** Attach an enforcement literal to every *group* of constraints that a human can act on (e.g. "room 104 unavailable on Thursday", "section X must be in building A", "capacity of room Y"), pass them as assumptions, solve with `num_workers=1` and no objective, and read `sufficient_assumptions_for_infeasibility()` ([assumptions sample](https://github.com/google/or-tools/blob/main/ortools/sat/samples/assumptions_sample_sat.py)). The core is not minimal; it is a candidate set.
2. **MUS minimisation (QuickXplain / deletion-based).** Junker's QuickXplain (AAAI 2004; correctness proof by [Rodler 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.01835)) shrinks the core to an irreducible one in O(k log(n/k)) solver calls; CPMpy ships deletion-based MUS, QuickXplain and optimal MUS on top of any assumption-capable solver, including CP-SAT ([CPMpy explain tools](https://cpmpy.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api/tools/explain.html); [CPMpy unsat cores](https://cpmpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/unsat_core_extraction.html)). Enumerating *several* MUSes (or a smallest one) gives the planner alternatives: "either relax A or relax B". MIP users get the same via Gurobi IIS; HiGHS's IIS is LP-only (§2.2).
3. **Soft relaxation with slack variables and lexicographic minimisation (always returns a schedule).** Give every hard-constraint family a non-negative slack (unplaced sections, over-capacity seats, double-booked slots, "wrong building" placements), minimise slacks lexicographically by family severity, then optimise preferences with the slack totals fixed. The output is a *minimal-violation timetable* plus the list of non-zero slacks — exactly the "why impossible and how to fix" artefact. This is the standard pattern in practice ([AMPL/OR forum pattern](https://groups.google.com/g/ampl/c/EzAjUj_CSzU); [Davison, Kheiri & Zografos 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jsched/v28y2025i2d10.1007_s10951-024-00817-w.html) use a lexicographic multi-objective binary program for hybrid-teaching timetables), and core-guided CP (Gange, Berg, Demirović & Stuckey, CPAIOR 2020) shows how cores can drive which soft constraints to relax ([CPAIOR 2020](https://cs.helsinki.fi/u/jezberg/papers/CPAIOR2020_Gange_Berg_Demirovic_Stuckey.pdf)).

**How products present it.** UniTime shows "Conflict-based Statistics" for unplaced classes — a list of the other assignments it collided with and why, noting that conflicts usually mean "too many classes competing over a fixed resource" ([UniTime solver manual](https://help.unitime.org/manuals/courses-solver)); aSc explains each red-cross conflict on double-click ([aSc help](https://help.asctimetables.com/text.php?id=538)); Timefold's score analysis / recommended-assignment APIs (now Plus) decompose the score per constraint and per entity. OptiChat (2023) wrapped IIS output in GPT-4 explanations and reported that both experts and non-experts found infeasibility sources faster ([Chen, Constante-Flores & Li 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12923)); TRACE-CS (AAAI 2025) uses SAT-derived explanations for *contrastive* queries ("why not room A at 10:30?") and has the LLM only paraphrase them ([TRACE-CS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.03671)). **Our explanation object should therefore be structured (constraint ids, entities, counts, suggested relaxations with their cost) and the LLM only renders it in Turkish/English.**

## 4. LLM + solver hybrids (2023–2026)

### 4.1 What the literature shows

* **NL → optimisation model agents:** NL4Opt competition (2023) started the field ([Ramamonjison et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08233)); OptiMUS (Stanford, 2023/2024) separates numerical data from prompts, builds a "connection graph" of clauses, writes and tests solver code per clause ([OptiMUS v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10172)); Chain-of-Experts (ICLR 2024) adds forward construction + backward reflection ([ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/d45ee77826332c100a1e15f7765b99ff-Paper-Conference.pdf)); the IJCAI 2025 survey by Xiao et al. covers fine-tuned models (ORLM, LLMOPT), multi-agent frameworks and benchmark quality, noting that NL4Opt itself has a ~26 % label error rate per an expert audit **[figure from a secondary summary; verify in the PDF]** ([survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.10047)). 2026 production-oriented systems: ORPilot (May 2026) uses an interview agent and a **solver-agnostic intermediate representation that is deterministically recompiled without LLM calls** to Gurobi/CPLEX/PuLP/Pyomo/OR-Tools ([ORPilot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02728)); OR-LLM-Agent (2025) targets Gurobi code ([OR-LLM-Agent](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.10009)).
* **Constraint programming specifically:** Michailidis, Tsouros & Guns show in-context learning for CPMpy models (CP 2024) ([LIPIcs](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol307-cp2024/LIPIcs.CP.2024.20/LIPIcs.CP.2024.20.pdf)); CP-Agent argues NL→CP needs an agentic loop with solver feedback (AGENT@ICSE 2026) ([CP-Agent](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07468)); Voboril & Szeider's agent improves existing CPMpy models while a validator guarantees equivalence ([arXiv 2608.08127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08127)); ConstraintLLM (EMNLP 2025) is a neuro-symbolic framework for industrial CP ([EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.809.pdf)); Mechqrane & Bessiere (JAIR 85, Feb 2026) do *active constraint acquisition* where the LLM interprets the user's natural-language answers to membership queries ([JAIR](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/19277)).
* **Scheduling-specific:** MeetMate (ACM TiiS 2024) had LLMs generate Python constraint functions for a CP meeting scheduler with high precision/recall against expert implementations and ran a 10-user study ([Lawless et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06908)); "Automated conversion of static to dynamic scheduler via natural language" uses Gemini + RAG over CP-SAT docs to patch a manpower-scheduling model when disruptions arrive ([arXiv 2405.06697](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06697)); U-Define studies user workflows for hard vs soft constraints in LLM planning ([arXiv 2605.02765](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02765)); VeriPlan adds formal verification to end-user planning ([arXiv 2502.17898](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.17898)).
* **No 2025–2026 paper was found that does end-to-end NL constraints → university timetable on real institutional data.** The closest is TRACE-CS (course *selection*, not room timetabling). I also could not find a Google Research paper on NL constraints for OR-Tools scheduling **[unverified; none surfaced]**.

### 4.2 Risks, quantified

* **Hallucinated or dropped constraints:** SCHEDBench (Aug 2026, 1,132 instances incl. ITC-2007 CB-CTT) finds average hard-feasibility of LLM-emitted schedules of **15.2 %** (GPT-5.5 55.9 %, Claude Opus 4.6 and Sonnet 4.6 among the 13 models), feasibility drops when constraints are merely *re-ordered*, and feasible answers have a 747 % mean optimality gap ([SCHEDBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00991v1)). ORAgentBench's best agent passes 35.5 % of tasks; failure modes are "missed operational rules, brittle formulations" ([ORAgentBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19787)). "When Must Becomes Maybe" documents hard constraints silently softened across agent stages ([arXiv 2608.24569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24569)). Component-level evaluation shows most errors are in constraint translation, not solving ([arXiv 2510.16943](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.16943)); a formal DSL that rejects malformed programs at parse time and verifies emitted schedules against the instance fixes much of this for small models ([arXiv 2608.18409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.18409)).
* **Reliability tooling:** Claude's structured outputs are GA: `output_config.format` (JSON schema) and `strict: true` tool definitions compile the schema into a grammar that constrains sampling, with limits (no recursive schemas, no numeric min/max, `additionalProperties: false`, ≤ 20 strict tools / request, grammar cached 24 h) ([structured outputs docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)). Schema validity ≠ semantic validity — the solver-side verifier is still required.

### 4.3 Recommended architecture: prompt → constraint diff → re-solve → explanation

1. **Grounding context.** Prompt contains the *catalogue* of entities (rooms with attributes, sections, departments, programmes, periods) as compact ids, the current timetable version id, and the list of existing constraints in IR form. Use prompt caching for the catalogue.
2. **Elicitation (Claude, strict tools).** One tool `propose_constraint_diff` whose input schema is our constraint IR: `{op: add|remove|relax, kind: enum[RoomUnavailable, PreferBuilding, SameRoom, MinGap, MaxDayLoad, FixAssignment, CapacityOverride, WeeksPattern, ...], scope: {sections|programme|instructor|room|...}, hardness: hard|soft(weight), weeks: bitstring, explanation_tr, explanation_en}`. Unknown entity names must come back as `ambiguities[]` for a clarifying question, never guessed. `strict: true` guarantees shape; a deterministic *compiler* turns IR into CP-SAT constraints (the ORPilot pattern — zero LLM calls after the IR).
3. **Preview & confirm.** Show the diff in plain Turkish/English (generated by the model from the IR, not from the user's text) and in a table; the admin confirms. This is where MeetMate's user study says preferences actually get formed.
4. **Re-solve in minimum-perturbation mode** (§5) with a short time limit and the previous solution as hint; run the **verifier** (independent Python checker over the full hard-constraint set, as SCHEDBench and R-ConstraintBench do with CP-SAT) before anything is shown ([R-ConstraintBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.15204)).
5. **If infeasible:** run the diagnostic ladder of §3; the LLM receives the structured explanation (MUS members, slack values, candidate relaxations with cost) and renders it, offering the "how to fix" options as *further constraint diffs* the user can accept.
6. **Audit:** persist prompt, IR diff, model id, verifier result and solver log with the timetable version.

Model choice: default `claude-opus-5-5` for elicitation and explanation (adaptive thinking, structured outputs, server-side refusal fallbacks available); `claude-haiku-5-5` is adequate for classification/routing of incoming free-text requests — confirm against the live model list at build time ([Claude structured outputs docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)).

## 5. Metaheuristics, LNS and minimum-perturbation re-scheduling

* **Where metaheuristics still win:** soft-score polishing on PE-CTT/CB-CTT (SA, tabu, ALNS — §1.1), exam proximity objectives (late acceptance, step-counting hill climbing — §1.3), and the ITC-2021 runner-up (multi-neighbourhood SA). Fix-and-optimize matheuristics (ITC-2019 winner; Lindahl, Sørensen & Stidsen 2018) are LNS with an exact sub-solver ([Lindahl et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jsched/v25y2022i4d10.1007_s10951-021-00715-5.html)); CP-SAT's own portfolio already runs LNS, feasibility-jump and violation-LS workers, so a *separate* metaheuristic layer is not needed initially. MaxSAT-based LNS has also been used for high-school timetabling ([Demirović & Musliu](https://dbai.tuwien.ac.at/user/demir/papers/MaxSAT%20Based%20Large%20Neighborhood%20Search%20for%20High%20School%20Timetabling-%20Demirovi%C4%87,%20Musliu.pdf)).
* **Minimum-perturbation problem (MPP).** Introduced for course timetabling by Müller, Rudová & Barták (PATAT 2004 / LNCS 2005) at Purdue — iterative forward search with conflict-based statistics minimising distance from the original solution ([MUNI record](https://www.ics.muni.cz/en/research/publications/621512)); Phillips, Walker, Ehrgott & Ryan (AOR 2017) resolve each infeasibility "in the smallest neighbourhood possible" with IP ([AOR 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/annopr/v252y2017i2d10.1007_s10479-015-2094-z.html)); Lemos, Monteiro & Lynce encode MPP in MaxSAT on ITC-2019 instances (CPAIOR 2020) and in two IP models with disruptions sampled from five years of IST history, solved incrementally (J. Sched 2021) ([EasyChair preprint](https://easychair.org/publications/preprint/29fw); [J. Sched 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jsched/v24y2021i1d10.1007_s10951-020-00666-3.html)); Akkan, Gülcü & Kuş (C&OR 2021, Turkish authors) minimise quality degradation subject to a bound on structural difference ([FSM record](https://acikerisim.fsm.edu.tr/items/59cd80f9-1cb1-426f-a202-a1fbbe6e9e95)). UniTime exposes this as "MPP mode" and as room-only/time-only change modes.
* **Implementation for us (CP-SAT):** keep `prev[s]` assignments; add Booleans `changed[s] = (room[s] ≠ prev_room[s]) ∨ (time[s] ≠ prev_time[s])`; lexicographic objective: (1) hard-slack = 0, (2) Σ weight·changed (weight higher for published/announced sections and for sections whose instructor/students are affected), (3) original soft score. Provide the previous solution via `add_hint`; for sub-second repairs after a manual drag-drop use `use_lns_only=True` with a 1–3 s limit and a *neighbourhood* limited to the touched day/building (the "Suggestions affecting ≤ 2 other classes" idea from UniTime).

## 6. Multi-week and exam specifics

* **Weeks patterns.** Encode as ITC-2019 week bitstrings per section (e.g. `11111110000000` for weeks 1–7) and add room/instructor disjunctions only for pairs with `weeks_a & weeks_b ≠ 0`; alternatively model each (section, week-block) as its own interval. Hybrid/online/UZEM modes and "% of theory remote" map to *reduced room demand*: a section with 30 % remote theory needs a room only in the weeks/meetings flagged face-to-face — Davison, Kheiri & Zografos (J. Sched 2025) give a lexicographic binary programme that treats each class as in-person, online or mixed and builds "demand-driven" timetables ([J. Sched 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jsched/v28y2025i2d10.1007_s10951-024-00817-w.html)).
* **Exam capacity halving.** UniTime distinguishes a room's *normal* capacity from its *examination seating* capacity and checks each exam against the appropriate one; the room-size penalty is the gap between the chosen capacity and enrolment ([UniTime exam manual](https://help.unitime.org/manuals/examination-timetabling)). Store `exam_capacity` per room (default ⌊capacity/2⌋, overridable per room/exam type) rather than hard-coding 50 %.
* **Multi-room exams ("2 large or 3 small").** UniTime gives each exam a *maximum number of rooms* and reports "Room Splits"; Infosilem Exam adds "striping" (several exams in one room) and a student-level conflict matrix ([Infosilem Exam](https://www.berger-levrault.com/ca/en/product/exam-scheduling-software-infosilem-exam/)). Model: for exam *e* with *n* students, Booleans `y[e,r]`, `Σ_r y[e,r]·examcap[r] ≥ n`, `Σ_r y[e,r] ≤ maxRooms[e]`, same period for all chosen rooms, optional "same building / adjacent rooms" soft term, and an `add_cumulative` over the invigilator pool with demand = number of rooms used (the 2022 MILP on eight Turkish public-university instances models invigilator workload fairness and consecutive-duty limits — a reusable second stage) ([Invigilator assignment, IJIE 2022](https://avesis.hacettepe.edu.tr/yayin/e570afe7-0f55-48f2-a487-341526a79485/invigilators-assignment-in-practical-examination-timetabling-problems)). No 2023–2024 paper was found that does split-rooms and invigilators in one IP; a 2026 three-stage MIP (days/slots → rooms → invigilators) exists **[found only via search summary; not read]**.
* **Student conflicts and gaps.** With Turkish programme/year cohorts, use the CB-CTT curriculum notion: hard = no two exams of the same programme-year in one period (exact ITC-2007 "exam conflict" when enrolment data are available); soft = two-in-a-row, two-in-a-day, period spread ≥ g, front-load large exams, with UniTime's "more than 2 a day" and "back-to-back distance" between buildings as additional terms ([ITC2007 exam report](https://www.eeecs.qub.ac.uk/itc2007/examtrack/report/Exam_Track_TechReportv4.0.pdf); [UniTime exam manual](https://help.unitime.org/manuals/examination-timetabling)). Bütünleme (resit) lists are a second exam problem with mostly fixed dates; model them as a separate problem instance sharing rooms, exactly as UniTime allows "multiple examination problems" per session.

## 9. Recommended 2026 roadmap

**Stack.** Python 3.12 service; OR-Tools CP-SAT 9.15 as the only solver (CP-SAT LNS workers for repair; MiniZinc models only in CI for cross-solver validation); a constraint IR (JSON Schema) compiled deterministically to CP-SAT; an independent pure-Python verifier; Claude API (`claude-opus-5-5` for elicitation/explanation with strict tool use and structured outputs; `claude-haiku-5-5` for cheap request triage) — re-check model ids and the strict-schema limits at implementation time. Persist timetables as versioned ITC-2019-style XML/JSON plus ICS export.

**Phases.**
1. *Weeks 1–3 — Data & model:* import existing request sheets and room inventory into the IR; implement the hard-constraint set (room no-overlap per week-pattern, instructor no-overlap, capacity, room type/TIP/PC, building/room requests as hard-or-soft per policy, availability windows 08:30–22:50 in 40-min slots); verifier; ITC-2019 validator-style self-check.
2. *Weeks 4–6 — Core solve:* full-term solve, room-only re-solve, lexicographic soft objective (requested room > requested building > utilisation balance > compactness); hints and `use_lns_only` repair; MPP objective.
3. *Weeks 7–9 — Infeasibility ladder:* enforcement literals per actionable constraint group, assumption core → QuickXplain MUS (CPMpy or own implementation), slack-relaxation diagnostic model, structured explanation object; UI "why/how to fix".
4. *Weeks 10–12 — Exams:* periods per exam type, exam seating capacity, multi-room split with maxRooms, cohort conflicts, soft spread terms, invigilator cumulative; bütünleme as second instance.
5. *Weeks 13–16 — NL layer:* constraint-diff tool schema, preview/confirm flow, explanation rendering (TR/EN), audit log; red-team with paraphrase and re-ordering tests (SCHEDBench-style).

**Key risks.** (1) Data quality — wrong capacities or missing instructor ids produce confident but wrong infeasibility reports (OR-Tools' own first advice is "check input data"); (2) assumption cores silently degrading when an objective is present — always run diagnostics on a feasibility-only copy with `num_workers=1`; (3) hint invalidation by presolve on re-solve — test `keep_all_feasible_solutions_in_presolve`; (4) LLM constraint drift/hallucination — never execute an IR diff that references an unknown entity, show the diff before solving, keep the verifier independent of both LLM and solver; (5) scope creep towards student-level sectioning (ITC-2019's hardest part) — our cohort-level model is sufficient for now; (6) Timefold-style "always returns something" expectations — our product must distinguish "feasible", "feasible with relaxations" and "infeasible, here is the MUS".

**Nice-to-have features reviewers will expect.** Scenario A/B diff with KPIs; utilisation heatmaps and "idle large rooms" alerts; travel-time between buildings A–D as a soft term (ITC-2019 `travel`); instructor preference and unavailability capture; "lock" (pin) assignments; publish/rollback with audit trail; ICS feeds per room/instructor/programme; batch "what if room X is closed for weeks 3–5" via the MPP solve; OOAPI-shaped read API for the student portal; a public "explain this placement" contrastive query ("why not B-204?") à la TRACE-CS.

**Evaluation.**
* *Hard feasibility (must be 100/100):* the verifier recomputes every hard constraint from the exported timetable and reports a count per family; CI fails on any non-zero. Also fuzz: randomly tighten capacities/availability until infeasible and assert that the MUS returned is (a) genuinely unsatisfiable when re-solved alone and (b) irreducible (removing any member makes it satisfiable).
* *Soft score:* report each lexicographic level separately plus a weighted total; track against the previous term's manual plan (same requests) — the comparison planners care about is "fewer unmet room/building requests, same or better utilisation, fewer changes".
* *Stability:* for a stream of N random edits, measure perturbation count and re-solve latency (target: p95 < 3 s for room-only, < 30 s for full MPP).
* *Benchmarks to run:* ITC-2019 early/middle instances through our model→XML path and the official validator (sanity that the model is expressive); ITC-2007 exam instances for the exam module; CB-CTT comp instances with the Udine validator for the soft-constraint engine (expect to match published optima on small instances).
* *Test data generation:* no 2023–2025 generator was found; the CB-CTT generator lineage (Burke et al. 2008 → Lopes & Smith-Miles 2010/2013 → De Coster et al. 2021 instance-space version) is the state of the art ([De Coster et al. 2022](https://air.uniud.it/retrieve/bc1f65f7-b01b-4a45-b69b-0f4728bec836/s10951-021-00701-x.pdf)). For us: build a parametric generator from our own term data (sections per department, slot-length mix, request distributions, week patterns), scale 0.5×–2×, and sample "disruption" scenarios from historical change logs as Lemos et al. did. Keep a frozen anonymised real term as the golden instance.

## Recommendations for this project

1. **Adopt the ITC-2019 data model wholesale** (days/weeks bitstrings, room options with penalties, distribution types, exam extensions from UniTime). It costs nothing and gives us a validator, public instances and a vocabulary planners can learn.
2. **Build the solver as three CP-SAT modes sharing one model builder:** `full` (lexicographic soft objective), `repair/MPP` (hints + perturbation objective + LNS-only short runs), and `diagnose` (feasibility copy with enforcement literals + MUS + slack relaxation). Never surface a timetable that the independent verifier has not passed.
3. **Treat the LLM as a translator and narrator, not a planner.** Strict-schema constraint diffs, deterministic compilation, mandatory preview, verifier gate, and structured explanations rendered in TR/EN. Budget a red-team eval (paraphrase, re-ordering, ambiguous room names) before launch.
4. **Model exams with exam-seating capacities, maxRooms splits and cohort conflicts from day one**; keep invigilators as a second-stage assignment.
5. **Copy the UX idioms planners already know:** UniTime's suggestions/CBS, aSc's colour-coded drop targets, Skedda's approval inbox, Infosilem's conflict matrix; add versioning + audit trail, which none of the open-source tools do well.
6. **Skip Timefold and MIP for v1**; revisit Timefold only if Java is already in the stack and real-time continuous planning becomes a requirement, and MIP only for lower bounds in reports.
